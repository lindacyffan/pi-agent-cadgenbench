## Working approach (one-shot generation)

Soft budget of about 15 minutes. Treat the entire CAD task as a single integrated generation problem: analyze the drawing, form one complete interpretation of the part, and generate the complete structure without a prescribed stage-by-stage workflow.

First, analyze the complete engineering drawing and establish an overall understanding of the part, including its major dimensions, geometric relationships, views, feature structure, and likely CAD construction strategy.

Then autonomously decide how to construct the model. You may choose the order in which you analyze information and organize the build123d code, but the initial geometry-changing `execute()` must represent your complete attempted model, including the body, major features, holes, pockets, bosses, walls, repeated features, and explicitly dimensioned details. Do not follow a predefined stage-by-stage decomposition supplied by the prompt. In particular, do not assume that the part must be generated in a fixed sequence such as body, then major features, then holes, then details.

You may autonomously decide:

- which drawing information to analyze first;
- which dimensions and constraints to prioritize;
- which geometric features to construct first;
- how to organize the build123d code;
- when to execute the model;
- when to validate or inspect the geometry;
- when to render or measure the model;
- which discrepancies to correct;
- and how many refinement iterations are necessary.

Maintain the relevant dimensions and geometric relationships throughout generation. Avoid losing or ignoring drawing information that has already been identified as important.

After constructing the initial model, inspect and validate it using the available tools. If discrepancies are found, revise the geometry and repeat the necessary inspection or execution steps. There is no artificial restriction on the number of validation, rendering, measurement, or refinement passes; use the tools as needed to obtain the most faithful final geometry.
