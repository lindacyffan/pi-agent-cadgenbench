## Working approach (one complete construction, then corrective iteration)
Soft budget of about 15 minutes — enough for verification and corrective refinement passes. Use it; do not stop at the first valid solid. All geometry you can justify from the drawing must be created by the first geometry-changing execute(); any later execute() is corrective recovery, not a planned staged sequence.

1. **Build a dimension table (read the drawing once).** Write out, as text: the overall envelope; every diameter and bolt circle; the full through-thickness profile from the section view (each thickness step, hub/boss height, counterbore and pocket depth); the count and positions of holes/pockets; and fillet/chamfer sizes. **Before the first execute(), classify every stacked dimension as either an overall span or a component span and write one arithmetic chain per affected axis** (for example `overall_height = base_height + body_height + boss_height`, so `body_height = overall_height - base_height - boss_height`). Determine each span from both extension-line endpoints in the view where it is dimensioned: explicitly name the geometry touched by the first and second endpoint. A dimension whose endpoints run from the part base to the top of a protruding boss already includes that boss and must not have its height added again; a dimension ending at the body roof excludes the boss. If an endpoint is visually ambiguous, write the two candidate chains and reject any candidate whose final bbox conflicts with that dimension in another orthographic view. Do not use a cavity depth or inferred roof thickness to redefine a printed overall span. A few targeted zoom-crops to read specific callouts are fine. Once the table is complete, stop re-reading the image for DIMENSIONS — but keep the isometric/section as a FORM reference (which way a curve arcs, where draft or taper runs, where a face is domed); do not flatten a clearly curved body into a constant-section extrusion just because the table is locked. Do NOT trace or extract the part's outline from the pixels (edge/contour detection, flood-fill, thresholding) — dimension and leader lines pollute it and it burns the budget; reconstruct the geometry parametrically from the printed dimensions instead. **Encode the table as named parameters, not inline numbers.** In your first execute(), bind every dimension from the table to a named top-level variable with a unit comment (`plate_thickness = 12.0  # mm`, `bolt_circle_dia = 80.0  # mm`, `hole_count = 6`), and build every feature by referencing those names — never re-type a raw dimension into a primitive, coordinate or Location. Derive dependent values by formula from the named ones (`hole_radius = clearance_dia / 2`) so a single transcription error cannot diverge two places. This changes only the code structure, never the geometry, but it eliminates arithmetic-slip errors and keeps every dimension checkable against your table in one place.

2. **Construct the complete part in the first execute().** The section/side view defines the Z-profile — that first execute() must create every thickness step, raised hub/boss, counterbore and pocket depth together with the complete hole pattern and every other plan feature. Do NOT extrude the plan outline at one uniform thickness unless the section genuinely shows a flat plate of constant thickness: a correct plan view on a wrongly-flattened body scores poorly on BOTH surface distance and volume. The section also reveals WALL THICKNESS — covers, housings, brackets and castings are usually thin-walled shells or cored bodies, not solid blocks; model the shell/cored body as part of this complete primary construction (offset/shell the outer form, or subtract an inner offset), because a thin-walled part built as a solid block leaves far too much material and tanks the volume-overlap score. Include the outer profile detail, central bore, hole pattern, bosses, walls, repeated features, and explicitly dimensioned cosmetic features in the same complete construction rather than checkpointing an intermediate profile and deferring plan features to later planned construction calls. After booleans, confirm with measure() (key dimensions), cross_sections(axis="Z") (through-thickness profile against your table) and find_bosses() (any hub/boss the drawing shows is present). **Before a boolean construction step you are not confident in** — fusing a boss/rib onto the base, cutting or trimming a feature back, anything where the obvious naive approach might leave a coincident face or remove more than intended — read the build123d://skill/modeling resource first. It is field-proven technique for exactly these failure modes (e.g. a boss fused on a coincident footprint silently failing to fuse, or a cut sized to a feature's rough extent removing adjacent material), not a generic hint; a guessed construction can pass validate() while still being the wrong shape or leaving avoidable topology problems that only show up later.

3. **Checkpoint.** Call validate(); as soon as it PASSES, export() AND save_snapshot() — a valid solid is now both on disk and recoverable in-session. If validate() FAILs, fix and re-validate before exporting.

4. **Dominant form correction gate.** After the first valid checkpoint, render the primary drawing views and identify the largest semantic mismatch between your model and the drawing. Do this even if bbox, hole count and validate() all pass: validity is not fidelity. Classify the body family first — thin-walled open cover/tray/housing, cored annular flange/housing, cast gearbox/pump housing with bosses and flanges, open spoked/ribbed/windowed casting, axisymmetric stepped part, folded sheet metal, organic/drafted casting, or simple prismatic block. If the rendered model is the wrong body family, rebuild the representation from the named dimension table; body-family correction means changing the primary construction strategy (shell, revolve, sweep, cored housing, drafted casting, open-spoke frame), not polishing a wrong mass. Fillets, chamfers, blends and small detail cuts do not count as dominant-form correction; use them only after the main volume, voids and silhouette match. If the first valid render is simplified/blocky or the body family is uncertain, read build123d://skill/modeling and use its body-family recipes before final export.

   Before final export, explicitly check that all of these are true: the body family matches the drawing; the section-derived thickness/profile is represented; major repeated features are present and not merged; holes/bores/bosses/mating interfaces still match recognizer counts after any fillet/chamfer pass; and the final render no longer has a known dominant mismatch such as too slab-sided, too blocky, through-open where the section shows a blind pocket, or merged vanes/lobes/spokes. If the render exposes one of these dominant mismatches, spend the next pass fixing that mismatch rather than adding cosmetic detail or finishing.

5. **Iterate to fidelity (several passes).** render_view() and compare against EACH drawing view (front, top, section, isometric). The isometric HIDES a flattened Z-profile — use render_view(clip_plane="y" or "x") to slice the model against the section view, and cross_sections(axis="Z") to confirm the relief (hubs, steps, bosses, counterbores) is present at the right depths; use measure() to check specific dimensions against your table. Name the largest remaining discrepancy, fix it with execute(), validate() again, and only when it PASSES re-export and re-snapshot, overwriting the checkpoint. Repeat until the renders match across all views. If the DOMINANT form is wrong — a curved sweep built as blocks, a revolve with square shoulders, a flat body where the section shows taper or draft — that IS the largest discrepancy: fix it before finishing, even though a curved/swept/lofted build risks the export gate. If your first attempt at the curved form fails validate(), try a SECOND construction (revolve or loft instead of sweep, or sweep a 2D section along a path) before falling back. Reverting to a dimensionally-correct but blocky checkpoint is a LAST resort, not the default.

6. **Accuracy pass before finishing.** measure() the overall envelope (X/Y/Z) and every dimensioned feature — each bore and bolt-circle diameter, the hole/pocket COUNT — and run cross_sections(axis="Z") to verify the through-thickness profile matches the section view (a flat area profile where the section shows steps or a hub means the part is flattened and wrong). Confirm each equals the drawing's printed value and fix any mismatch. Bores, mating faces and bolt-hole positions are scored on their exact size and location — place them precisely, and settle these before adding cosmetic fillets/chamfers.
